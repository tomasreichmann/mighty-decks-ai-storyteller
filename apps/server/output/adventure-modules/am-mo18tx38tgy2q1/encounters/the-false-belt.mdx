## GM Intent

This is a high-pressure ship encounter about realizing the battlefield is lying. The crew jumps into what looks like an asteroid field, discovers many of the rocks are missile turret drones in disguise, and must either escape the belt or destroy enough of the ambush to salvage useful parts.

![](/api/adventure-artifacts/the-false-belt-fa687fd1ee5fe3d76c8f.png)

# The False Belt

*The Spin Drive snaps shut behind you and the stars return as hard points of light. Too many of them move.*

*Asteroids fill the forward view. The ship drops into the middle of a tumbling belt with almost no warning: jagged rock above, below, ahead, behind. Proximity alarms scream. The cockpit glass strobes with collision vectors.*

*Then one of the nearest asteroids opens.*

*Stone-colored plates peel back from a metal iris. A targeting optic burns green inside the rock. Another "asteroid" rotates against the field's drift, unfolding missile rails from a shell of dust and slag. Then a dozen more wake up at once.*

*The belt is not a belt. It is an ambush.*

* The real asteroids are still dangerous and can crush the ship if the pilot takes a bad vector.
* The fake asteroids are camouflaged missile turrets with short-burn pursuit drives.
* The turrets can launch missiles, reposition through the field, and chase the corvette until the crew breaks lock or destroys them.
* Sensors show a narrow escape lane, but it bends through the densest part of the field.

**The crew has seconds before the first missile wave reaches them.**

## Encounter clock

Use a counter based on the crew's chosen goal.

### Escape the kill box

> Counter: <GameCard type="CounterCard" slug="clear-the-false-belt" /> (2x players)
> Threat: Missile impacts, collision damage, and system strain
> Reward: The ship escapes with navigation data and salvage coordinates

Each round, every player describes how they help the ship survive the field. Good approaches include:

* Pilot through a rotating gap before it closes.
* Feed the cockpit cleaner vectors from the sensor array.
* Overdrive engines for a short burst without tearing them apart.
* Jam missile locks with comms static, chaff, vented coolant, or a decoy beacon.
* Fire the laser array at missiles, exposed turret housings, or loose asteroids.
* Load or improvise a missile if the bay is operational.
* Brace damaged rooms, vent heat, reroute power, or seal micro-breaches.

At the end of each round, if the crew has not cleared the counter, the missile asteroids escalate:

1. **Missile wave:** Defend against 1 Effect per player. Missing Effect becomes ship-system Damage or player Injury if someone is exposed in the struck room.
2. **Collision vector:** The safest path runs through tumbling real rock. Missing Effect damages Engines, Cockpit, Sensors, or the hull-facing room most involved in the action.
3. **Pursuit burn:** Several fake asteroids ignite hidden drives and chase. Increase the remaining counter by 1 unless someone breaks lock, blinds sensors, or destroys a pursuer.

If the crew clears the counter, read:

*The corvette punches through a gap that was not there a heartbeat ago. Missiles bloom behind you, fooled into the real rocks. The false belt collapses into fire and spinning debris as the ship claws its way into open space.*

## Optional objective: destroy the missile asteroids

If the crew chooses to fight instead of only escaping, add or switch to this counter.

> Counter: <GameCard type="CounterCard" slug="break-the-ambush" /> (3x players)
> Threat: The longer the crew stays, the more turret asteroids wake up
> Reward: Better salvage, missile parts, and intelligence about whoever seeded the belt

Destroying fake asteroids requires exposing them, pinning their movement, or forcing their missiles back into them. Effects can be spent one-for-one to:

* Destroy a missile before it hits.
* Crack open a turret shell and expose its core.
* Disable a turret's pursuit drive.
* Make a fake asteroid collide with a real one.
* Salvage a mostly intact launcher after the fight.

If the crew reaches half the counter, they identify the command logic: each turret follows a simple ambush protocol and only updates targets when the sensor lattice flashes green. A clever crew can exploit that timing for advantage.

If the crew clears **Break the Ambush**, read:

*The last false asteroid tries to run. Its stone shell shears away under your fire, revealing a compact missile bus wrapped in old stealth plating. It tumbles end over end, venting sparks, then dies cold. For the first time since the jump, the field is only rock.*

## Consequences

* **Clean escape:** The crew clears the field and takes no more than one system Damage. They keep the route data and can avoid this ambush pattern in future jumps.
* **Costly escape:** The crew clears the field but suffers multiple hits. They escape, but the Storyteller should mark damaged systems and make repairs matter before the next dangerous jump.
* **Failed escape:** The ship does not get destroyed outright. It is forced into a rough emergency micro-jump or hides inside the shadow of a large real asteroid, losing time and taking serious system Damage.
* **Total victory:** The crew both clears the field and destroys the missile asteroids. They gain the better reward package below.

## Rewards

### Reward for clearing the field

* The crew records a safe lane through this part of the belt. Future travel through this route does not trigger the ambush again.
* The sensor array stores a signature for camouflaged missile rocks. The next time the crew encounters hidden automated weapons, the first scan starts with +1 useful information.
* Salvage coordinates mark several destroyed turret shells for later recovery.

### Extra rewards for destroying the missile asteroids

* Gain <GameCard type="AssetCard" slug="missile-parts" /> worth enough to build or reload **2 missiles** in the ship's missile bay.
* Gain one intact <GameCard type="AssetCard" slug="decoy-beacon" />: launch or plant it to make a missile, drone, or automated turret prefer the beacon for one exchange.
* Recover <GameCard type="AssetCard" slug="stealth-plating-scraps" />: enough material for one ship repair, one sensor-masking project, or one valuable trade with smugglers or Machinists.
* Decode a partial maker's mark from the turret firmware. The ambush was not random; someone seeded this route and expected ships with working Spin Drives to pass through.

## Storyteller notes

* Keep the scene focused on ship roles. Cockpit, Sensors, Engines, Weapons, Missile Bay, Reactor, and Life Support should all feel relevant.
* The fake asteroids are machines, not living enemies. They do not negotiate, panic, or retreat unless their protocol tells them to preserve hardware.
* Do not hide the reveal for long. The fun is reacting to the lie under pressure, not slowly proving it.
* If the crew is already badly damaged, make escape possible with clever play and put the best salvage behind the optional destruction objective.
