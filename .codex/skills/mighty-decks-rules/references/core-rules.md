# Core Mighty Decks Rules

## Design Philosophy

- Narrative consequence over randomness.
- Escalation instead of stagnation.
- Player agency in failure.
- Alternating tension for pacing.
- Low cognitive overhead.

## Table Roles And Components

- 1 Storyteller and 1-4 players.
- Each player has an Outcome Deck and chooses 2 Stunts during setup.
- Components include Outcome cards, Stunt cards, Effect cards, Counter cards, and dice used as trackers.

## Outcome Deck

Each player deck has 12 cards:

- 1 Special Action
- 3 Success
- 3 Partial Success
- 4 Fumble
- 1 Chaos

Players draw 3 at start. If all 3 are Fumbles, redraw.

## Outcome Values

| Card | Effect | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Success | +2 | Clean success |
| Partial Success | +1 | Success with complication |
| Fumble | -1 | Something goes wrong |
| Chaos | variable | Unpredictable; not valid for defense |
| Special Action | +3 | Only with Stunt, expertise, or Storyteller approval |

Effect value represents progress, damage, persuasion intensity, or consequence severity.

## Actions

1. Player describes intent.
2. Storyteller establishes stakes: success result and failure result.
3. Player plays an Outcome card only if risk and meaningful consequences exist.
4. Storyteller interprets Effect narratively.

## Catastrophe

- If a player holds 3 or more Fumbles, reveal them and trigger a Catastrophe.
- Storyteller narrates dramatic failure.
- Player discards hand and redraws 3 cards.

## Defense

- A threatened player may play an Outcome card to defend.
- Chaos cannot defend.
- Effect value reduces incoming harm.
- If no defense is played, full consequences apply.

## Effects

- Injury: 4 Injury means Dying; while Dying, only Defend or Sacrifice.
- Distress: 3 Distress means Panicked and must play top card; 4 Distress means Hopeless and cannot act.
- Boost: +1 Effect or extra move; discard after use.
- Complication: -1 Effect; may be discarded by playing a Partial Success.
- Hindered: cannot move and act.
- Stuck: cannot move.
- Freezing: at 2, gain Distress.
- Burning: at 2, gain Injury.

## Stunts And Counters

- Stunts represent talents, signature moves, background advantages, and unique abilities.
- Stunts enable Special Actions and may modify Effect values.
- Counters track progress, doom, collapse, rituals, time pressure, environmental threats, or scene goals.
- Counters should create forward momentum when players succeed, fail, or time advances.

## Scenes

- High tension scenes include combat, moral dilemmas, chases, and crises; turn-based play may activate.
- Low tension scenes cover travel, roleplay, investigation, and downtime; use freeform narrative play.
- End a scene when a goal is achieved, a threat resolves, or the situation changes dramatically.
- Effects persist into the next scene.

## Sessions And Adventures

- End a session at a major turning point, cliffhanger, or emotional resolution.
- Finishing an adventure should reflect character growth, lasting injuries, moral consequences, and changed relationships.
